Creative inspiration & portfolio platform

Three homepage layouts for a community where creatives post images and motion work, follow each other and use their profile as a portfolio. These are structural wireframes. Grey boxes are content, red numbers point to the design notes under each frame. Visual style, brand and colour come later.

Direction: A × C hybrid

The feed width and simple header of A, plus the sense of belonging from C. The sidebar becomes a slim icon rail, so the feed gets its full width back. Interest rings replace the chip row, so there is one way to switch topics instead of two. C's right-rail community modules move into the feed as breaks (A's pattern) and come back as a right rail only on very wide screens.

Option A: Feed-first masonry

The feed is the homepage. Content starts within 150px of the top and goes edge to edge, with as little chrome as possible. Community shows up as breaks inside the feed instead of a separate column. This is the closest to the Pinterest habit loop (scroll, save, scroll) and the easiest layout to take to mobile.

Option B: Editorial showcase

The homepage reads like a curated magazine. A hero sets out the value, then sections take turns showing work, motion, people and community activity. This layout is portfolio-forward: creators and project quality are the story. It works best as the logged-out landing page and is the strongest option for SEO and sign-ups.

Option C: Community hub (app shell)

A three-column app layout for signed-in members. Navigation and the user's own collections sit on the left, the feed is in the centre, and a right rail holds the community: the challenge, trending tags and who to follow. The interest row is borrowed from stories: a ring means the topic has new work since your last visit. Of the three options, this one feels most like a place you belong to.

Motion playback rules
  • Autoplay in view. Plays muted and loops once at least 60% of the card is visible. It pauses and goes back to the poster frame when it leaves the viewport.
  • Never a real GIF. Uploads are transcoded to a 6 to 8 second, roughly 500 KB MP4/WebM preview. It looks like a GIF, but files are about 10 times smaller and the quality is better.
  • Concurrency cap. At most 3 previews play at once, nearest the centre of the screen first. The rest show the poster frame plus the badge.
  • Reduced motion and data saver. If the OS has reduced motion on, or the user is on data saver, nothing autoplays. Cards show the poster with a play button.
  • Hover. Shows the length, a sound toggle and Save. Hovering does not unmute, because sound only plays after the user chooses it.
  • Click. Opens the detail modal. The full-length video plays from the start with sound and controls. The URL updates so the piece can be shared.
  • Badge. A "play" pill shows on every motion card, even while it is playing, so motion stays recognisable in a still screenshot or a paused feed.
  • Upload. Creators choose the poster frame and trim the preview loop. Without this, the first frame (often black) is what people see.

Chosen: A × C hybrid for members (B kept as the logged-out landing)

Show the homepage based on who is looking at it:

  1. Logged-out → Option B. Its job is to explain the product, prove the quality of work on it, and turn visitors into members. It also gives search engines rich, crawlable sections.
  2. Logged-in → A × C hybrid. The full-width feed from A with the navigation, interest rings, challenge and activity from C. See the first tab.
  3. One responsive system. The community rail appears only at 1536px and up. Below that it moves into the feed as breaks, and on mobile the rail becomes a bottom tab bar.
  4. Shared card and motion spec everywhere. Motion is the feature that sets this apart, so it behaves the same on every surface.

Next steps: map the user flows (onboarding interest picker, upload, profile and portfolio), then a mid-fidelity pass on the chosen layout.
